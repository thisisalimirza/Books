In 1784, a famine reached Oudh severely enough that even the nobility were
reduced to penury. The Nawab, Asaf-ud-Daula, responded by commissioning the
Bara Imambara in Lucknow and putting more than twenty thousand people to
work on it.

During the day, laborers built. Then, on the night of every fourth day, he
hired a second crew to tear down part of what the first had raised.

Build. Destroy. Build again.

The point was never the building. It was that the work continued, and that
people ate.
