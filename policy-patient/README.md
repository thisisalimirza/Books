## Logline

How well-intentioned health policy ends up harming the patients it's meant to
help — and a proposed index for predicting, before a policy ships, how much
patient harm it's likely to cause.

## Where it stands

Just scaffolded. Next step is fleshing out `outline.md`: which policies serve
as case studies, and what the index actually measures.

## Working thesis

Policies aimed at a system-level goal (cost, access, quality metrics) often
degrade outcomes for the patients closest to the mechanism, because the
harm is diffuse, delayed, or falls on people with the least ability to
object. The book argues this is predictable, not incidental — and proposes
an index (working name: the **Policy Patient Index**) that scores a proposed
policy on its likely patient-level harm before it's enacted, the way an
environmental impact statement scores ecological harm before a project
breaks ground.
