# Im Changing My Mind on Minimum Wage

For a long time I treated minimum wage hikes the way a lot of people in tech and finance do: as a well-intentioned policy that mostly just raises prices, reduces hours, or pushes jobs toward automation. The data seemed clear enough. Raise the floor too high and the people you’re trying to help end up with fewer opportunities. Free-market economics, right?

I’ve started to change my mind. Not on the core economics, but on what we should actually do while those economics play out in the real world.

Here’s the specific idea I’ve been turning over: raise the minimum wage only for companies with more than 50 employees or more than 10 franchises/independent locations. Leave the true small businesses alone.

Larger companies have the scale, the margins, and the pricing power to absorb higher labor costs if they choose to. Small local shops do not. This creates a natural choice for workers. You can take the higher pay and the more corporate environment, or you can take lower pay and the closer, more human scale of a neighborhood business. Both options stay viable.

The immediate objection is always the same: “They won’t absorb the cost. They’ll just pass it on to consumers.”

Okay. And?

First, it’s not a foregone conclusion that every large company will pass every dollar through. Plenty of scaled businesses have absorbed cost increases in the past when the alternative was worse PR, worse retention, or worse culture. Some will do it again.

Second, even if the net real purchasing power doesn’t improve much in the end, the average person who is less financially literate still *feels* better. Subjective experience matters. Feeling like your wage is going up is not nothing, especially when so much of modern economic discussion treats people as pure rational actors staring at CPI charts.

Third, and this is the part that actually shifted me: millions of Americans are not just failing to benefit from economic growth. They are actively being hurt by the way that growth is currently distributed. Look at the data-center boom. The average person is not using AI to compound capital or find higher-leverage work. They’re using it to make memes and write LinkedIn slop. Yet many of them are watching their utility bills climb to power infrastructure they don’t meaningfully use. I don’t necessarily share the pure “data center hate,” but I completely understand the frustration.

The core diagnosis is detachment. Labor has become disconnected from the asset side of the economy. My stock portfolio can be ripping higher while I walk past more homeless people than I’ve ever seen. That is not a stable equilibrium, and pretending the only acceptable response is “wait for perfect market solutions” feels increasingly tone-deaf.

Raising the minimum wage at scale companies will not solve the deeper problem. I know that. It might only improve outcomes for a relatively small percentage of workers. But that small percentage is still millions of actual human lives. Those people do not have the privilege of sitting on a comfortable couch waiting for the ideal policy. They need some form of relief now.

I’m not abandoning free-market reasoning. I’m just no longer willing to treat “this won’t fix everything” as a reason to do nothing. Sometimes the less-perfect intervention that helps real people today is better than the theoretically pure non-intervention that leaves them waiting indefinitely.

That’s the shift.
