# What Poverty Really Means

The poorest people I’ve met weren’t in slums.  
They were in suits—shrinking from their own potential.

That sentence has taken me years to be able to say out loud.

Because it sounds cruel.  
Because it sounds privileged.  
Because it sounds like something someone who’s never truly seen poverty might say.

But I have.  
I’ve lived in five countries.  
I’ve seen extreme absolute poverty and extreme wealth up close — sometimes just blocks apart.  
And the older I get, the more I realize: poverty isn’t just about lack of money.  
It’s about **a mindset that trains people to stay small.**

---

### Material Poverty is Real — But It’s Not the Whole Picture

Let me be clear: growing up poor is hard. Unfair. Systemic.

It robs people of choices. Of safety. Of time.  
It burdens kids with the weight of adult stress before they can even name it.

I don’t romanticize that. I’ve seen what it does.

But what I want to talk about is something adjacent — something more dangerous because it doesn’t *look* like poverty.

It looks like:

- Smart people who stop trying once they land a stable job.
- Talented kids who believe that “getting out” is the same as “getting free.”
- Adults who spend more time defending their limits than pushing them.
- Entire institutions that select for conformity and punish ambition.

This isn’t financial poverty.  
It’s **possibility poverty**.

And it’s everywhere.

---

### Maslow’s Trap

Everyone knows Maslow’s hierarchy: first you meet your basic needs — food, shelter, safety — then you work your way up to self-actualization.

It sounds reasonable. But it’s a trap.

What it assumes is that people climb the pyramid automatically once they’re safe.  
But they don’t.  
Most people get stuck in the lower rungs — not because they can’t move up, but because they don’t even remember what wanting more felt like.

I’ve seen people making six figures with healthcare, housing, and family support — and they’re still living like the goal is to not lose what they’ve got.

That’s not safety. That’s learned helplessness with a paycheck.

The worst part is: they call it “being realistic.”

---

### Scarcity Isn’t a Budget — It’s a Frame

Scarcity mindset isn’t about how much you have.  
It’s about how you **think**.

It’s the belief that:

- Opportunities are rare, so you must guard yours.
- Money is hard to make, so don’t spend it learning.
- Ideas are dangerous, so stick to the script.
- Your value comes from proving you’re not a burden.

I’ve seen people who grew up poor and broke out of it — only to keep living by those same rules. They don’t build. They hoard. They don’t experiment. They preserve.

They act like they’re still in danger — even when they’re not.

And ironically, that mindset *creates* the very fragility they fear.

---

### The Abundance Shift

You know when someone has made the shift.  
You can feel it.

They act with spaciousness.  
They think in decades.  
They share ideas freely.  
They learn out loud.  
They invest in people without needing immediate ROI.

Abundance isn’t recklessness.  
It’s confidence in the long game.

And it almost never happens by accident.

Someone has to choose to *believe* in growth before they can act like it.

---

### Systems > Suffering

This is the hardest lesson I had to learn:

> **Working harder inside a broken system doesn’t make you strong. It makes you tired.**

I used to believe I could outwork problems. That brute force effort was the noble path.

Then I started studying systems.

That changed everything.

Because once you understand systems — economic, social, institutional — you start seeing how much of “personal failure” is really just **bad architecture**.

And then you realize:

If the game is rigged, you can keep playing — or you can learn to build better games.

Most people stay poor because they never question the game.  
They just try harder.

---

### Staying Small Is a Choice (After a Certain Point)

If you’ve made it past survival…  
If you have time to read essays…  
If you have internet, a phone, and enough margin in your life to wonder about what comes next…

Then let me say this directly:  
**Staying small is no longer something that’s being done to you.**  
It’s a choice you’re making.

No one is coming to grant you permission.  
No one is going to tell you you’re ready.  
No one is going to pick you.

You have to build your way out.  
Build your systems.  
Build your leverage.  
Build your sense of identity beyond your job or your GPA or your LinkedIn bio.

And if that scares you — good.  
It should.

Because what you do next is no longer about escaping poverty.  
It’s about refusing to pass it on.

---

### Final Thought: Don’t Just Escape the System — Replace It

The goal isn’t just to “make it out.”  
The goal is to **build something so strong that others can walk through it too.**

I want to build schools, hospitals, and companies — not because it sounds good in a pitch deck — but because I’ve seen what happens when people never get the chance.

I don’t just want to make money.  
I want to **redistribute belief**.

I want kids in Pakistan and Kenya and South Central to grow up knowing they don’t need to beg the world to take them seriously.  
They just need to **build something useful**.  
And then another.  
And another.

We don’t fix poverty with pity.  
We fix it with power.
